The Four Variants of FIRE

1. Lean FIRE

  • Definition: FI with minimal spending (typically $25-40k/year)
  • FI number: $625k - $1M (25x of $25-40k)
  • Lifestyle: Frugal, often single or without children, low cost-of-living area
  • Commitment: Less room for variable expenses, more lifestyle restrictions

2. Fat FIRE

  • Definition: FI with comfortable to luxurious spending ($100-250k+/year)
  • FI number: $2.5M - $6.25M+
  • Lifestyle: Travel, hobbies, restaurants, premium healthcare
  • Commitment: Takes much longer to reach; requires a high income or a long timeline

3. Coast FIRE

  • Definition: Saved enough that, WITHOUT new contributions, compound growth reaches traditional FI by age 65
  • Formula: Número Coast FIRE = Número FI / (1+r)^anos_ate_aposentadoria_tradicional
  • Example: $1.5M FI at 65 / (1.07)^30 = $197k needed at age 35
  • After Coast: Only enough to cover current expenses; optional contributions
  • Commitment: Still working, but with enormous flexibility

4. Barista FIRE

  • Definition: Part-time work covers ongoing expenses; the portfolio grows untouched OR provides partial income
  • FI number: Often 50-70% of the traditional FI number
  • Lifestyle: Part-time job (often for health benefits), portfolio supplements income
  • Commitment: Still doing some work, but low-stress and by choice

Calculation Engine

FI Number Calculation

Número FI Tradicional = Gasto Anual × 25  (assume SWR de 4%)
FI Conservador = Gasto Anual × 28-33  (SWR de 3.0-3.5% para linha do tempo de 50+ anos)
FI Agressivo = Gasto Anual × 20-22  (SWR de 4.5-5% para linha do tempo mais curta)

Years to FIRE formula (with existing balance)

Anos = ln((FI - PV×(1-r)/PMT + PMT/r) / (PMT/r)) / ln(1+r)
Onde:
  FI = alvo do número de FI
  PV = carteira atual
  PMT = poupança anual
  r = taxa de retorno real

Coast FIRE Calculation

Número Coast FIRE = Número FI Tradicional / (1 + r)^(idade_aposentadoria_tradicional - idade_atual)

Use r = 5-7% real returns.

Sequence-of-Returns Risk

The first 5 years of retirement matter disproportionately. If the market drops 30% in year 1 and you withdraw 4%, you’ve effectively withdrawn 5.7% of the original amount — recovery is much harder.

Mitigation strategies:

  1. Bond tent: Keep 3-5 years of expenses in bonds/cash near retirement
  2. Cash cushion: 1-2 years of living expenses in HYSA
  3. Flexible expenses: Cut variable expenses in down years
  4. Safety Rails (Guyton-Klinger): Adjust withdrawals based on portfolio performance
  5. Part-time income: Even a small income (Barista FIRE) cushions downturns

Withdrawal Strategies

Strategy 1: The 4% Rule (Trinity Study)

  • Withdraw 4% of the initial portfolio, increasing it by inflation each year
  • 30-year success rate: ~95% (60/40 portfolio)
  • 50-year success rate: ~85%
  • Simple, well studied

Strategy 2: The 3.5% Rule (Early Retirement Adjustment)

  • For retirements 50+ years away
  • More conservative, higher success rate
  • Adds $$ to the FI number (Annual × 28.5)

Strategy 3: Dynamic Withdrawal (as a % of the current portfolio)

  • Withdraw a fixed percentage (e.g., 4%) of the CURRENT portfolio each year
  • Never runs out, but income varies
  • Recommendation: guardrails with a floor and ceiling

Strategy 4: Guyton-Klinger Guardrails

  • Initial: 5% withdrawal
  • If the portfolio falls 20% below the initial path → cut withdrawals by 10%
  • If the portfolio rises 20% above the initial path → increase withdrawals by 10%
  • Allows a higher upfront fee with a safety mechanism

Strategy 5: Bucket Strategy

  • Bucket 1: 1-2 years in cash
  • Bucket 2: 3-7 years in bonds
  • Bucket 3: 8+ years in stocks
  • Replenish stocks in good years, spend cash in bad years
