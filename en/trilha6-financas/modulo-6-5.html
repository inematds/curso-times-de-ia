Common Pitfalls to Avoid

  • Do not ignore taxes — The choice between Roth and Traditional changes completely depending on the future vs. current tax rate
  • Do not ignore transaction costs — Buying a home incurs selling costs of ~6% later
  • Do not count appreciation twice — If you assume 7% returns, don't also assume 7% real estate appreciation (real estate is closer to 3-4% over the long term)
  • Do not ignore behavioral factors — Forced savings through a mortgage may outperform "invest the difference" if the user doesn’t actually invest
  • Do not assume linear costs — Cars depreciate nonlinearly; homes have irregular maintenance costs

Scenario-Specific Models

Buy vs. rent

Track: mortgage principal and interest, property tax, insurance, maintenance, opportunity cost of the down payment, versus rent + investing the down payment. Tipping point: years spent in the home. Less than 5 years generally favors renting.

Pay Off Mortgage vs. Invest

Compare the mortgage APR (after the tax deduction) with the expected market return. If the mortgage is 7% and the expected return is 7%, the guaranteed return wins.

Roth vs. Traditional

Compare your current marginal tax rate with the expected rate in retirement. Higher now → Traditional. Lower now → Roth.

New Job vs. Staying

Factor: pay gap, stock compensation, retirement match, benefits cost difference, commute, career trajectory.
