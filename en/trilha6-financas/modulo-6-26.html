Sequence of Returns Risk Mitigation

In the 5 years before AND after the FIRE date:

  1. Build 2-3 years of living expenses in cash/HYSA
  2. Keep 5-7 years of expenses in bonds (medium-term)
  3. Plan flexible vs. essential spending (cut variable expenses in down years)
  4. Consider the Barista phase as a bridge during the first few years
  5. Do not sell stocks in bear markets — spend cash/bonds

Roth Conversion Ladder (Tax Hack for Early Retirees)

Access to retirement funds before age 59.5 without a 10% penalty:

  1. Transfer Traditional 401k → Traditional IRA in year 1 of FIRE
  2. Convert $X/year from Traditional IRA → Roth IRA (taxed in lower income brackets)
  3. After the 5-year waiting period, withdraw the converted amount from the Roth without penalty
  4. Live on taxable funds + Roth already converted during the waiting years

Health Strategy (Pre-65)

  • ACA Exchange: Plan income to maximize subsidies (manage MAGI)
  • HSA: Maximum contributions during working years ($X/year); keep receipts for tax-free withdrawals decades later
  • Health Sharing Ministries: Not safe, but a lower-cost option for healthy individuals
  • Barista FIRE for benefits: Starbucks, REI, Costco, UPS offer health insurance to temporary workers

Action Plan

This Month

  1. Calculate your actual current savings rate (use net income and count all savings)
  2. Identify $500/month in spending cuts → increase savings rate by X%
  3. Open a Roth IRA if you don't already have one (top tax-advantaged priority for FIRE)

This Quarter

  1. Optimize tax-advantaged account sequence: 401k match → HSA → Roth IRA → max out 401k → taxable
  2. Plan a move for geographic arbitrage (if applicable)
  3. Build the first month of cash buffer

This Year

  1. Increase savings rate by 5+ percentage points
  2. Review allocation for an appropriate stock/bond split for FIRE
  3. Read: “The Simple Path to Wealth” (Collins), the “Early Retirement Now” SWR series

Risks and Items to Watch

  • Sequence of returns in the first 5 years after FIRE
  • Healthcare cost overruns (biggest FIRE risk)
  • Long-term care need
  • Tax law changes (Roth treatment, capital gains rates)
  • Sustained inflation above 3%
  • Lifestyle inflation reversing your math
  • Loss of identity/community when work stops (plan what you’re "retiring to," not just what you’re "retiring from")
